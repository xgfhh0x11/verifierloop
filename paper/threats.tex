Section~4 asked how a procedure can be evaluated at all. This section asks what
could make the specific claims of Section~5 wrong, and what would settle each.

\paragraph{The population's frame is biased, and the direction is measured.}
Section~3.2 states two requirements on the population. The first---independence from
any oracle's findings---is satisfied. The second is not: the fifty-seven fixes were
selected because their messages quote internal state, which is what makes them
readable. That property may correlate with how a defect was found and reported, so
the frame is not a random sample of the component's defect history.

The held-out work of Section~4.1 bounds the bias, because its frame was drawn from
the complement of that filter---fixes in the same component whose messages do
\emph{not} quote internal state---restricted, for buildability, to fixes since 2023.
In the filtered frame, 25 of 57 defects lie on the state-equivalence path; in the
complement frame, 3 of 20 ($p = 0.030$, Fisher's exact, two-sided). The comparison
confounds the filter with the window: the flagship frame carries no date cut and runs
from 2016 to 2026. Restricted to the 22 flagship fixes since 2023, 8 lie on the path,
against 3 of 20 in the complement; the point estimate is still about 2.4-fold, but at
these sizes it is not significant ($p = 0.17$). Selecting on messages that quote
verifier state over-represents state-equivalence-path defects; the size of the
enrichment is measured on unequal windows and should be read as roughly two- to
threefold.

This does not overturn the figures in Section~5.3; it corrects what they are
fractions of. It also exposes an asymmetry between the two requirements of
Section~3.2 that the text there does not anticipate. The first---independence from
findings---was kept, and it protects the bound's logic. The second---a frame not
correlated with visibility---was broken, and it was broken in the direction that
inflates exactly the quantity reported: a frame selected on printed verifier state is
a frame selected on the oracle's own inputs. The two requirements are separable in
principle; in this application the one that failed was the one whose failure flatters
the number. The 42\% blind and 2\% targeted are conditional on a frame enriched
for the very path the oracle operates on, and on the complement frame both shares
fall. What the procedure delivers unconditionally is the classification of a given
population, not a population-independent constant---which is Section~3.4's point,
arriving here as a measurement rather than a caution.

\paragraph{The frame excludes the calibration pairs, in the direction that flatters the
number.}
The sixty-eight fixes matching the frame's pattern include eleven of the sixteen
calibration pairs, and the pairs were removed before classification because they had
already been used to calibrate the oracles. Two of the eleven are predicate defects the
oracle is calibrated to see. The exclusion therefore lowers the targeted share and
raises the blind one: with the pairs restored the oracle can target 3 of 68 rather than
1 of 57, and its realized reach is 2 of 68 rather than 0 of 57. The blind share on the
restored frame was not re-rated; the eleven pairs are classified in Appendix~B by the
oracle each anchors, not by the step of Section~3.3.

\paragraph{Both partitions were produced with model assistance, and reproducibility is
measured between two model raters, not humans.}
Classification was carried out with automated help: the first rater is the author's own
model-assisted session, the second an independent model instance given only the
question, the consumption set and the diffs. The coefficients therefore measure
agreement between two model readers of the same diffs. Only the decision-changing
case of the flagship partition was re-derived by hand. Section~4.5 supplies
inter-rater measurements on both the held-out cases and the rebuilt flagship
partition; the original forty could not be re-rated because its list was not
retained (Section~4.2). Its result cuts both
ways for this threat. Agreement was substantial on the held-out cases ($\kappa =
0.71$) and near-perfect on the rebuilt flagship partition ($\kappa = 0.86$), so neither
partition is an idiosyncrasy of one reader; but on the held-out cases the second rater
used \emph{Undecided} where the first never did, and the disagreements clustered on a
seam in the step's own wording.
A human inter-rater study over the full population remains the experiment that would
settle it.

\paragraph{One oracle was modified after a failed run.}
The visible held-out case fired only after the oracle's era routing was corrected
(Section~4.1). The modification was to routing, not to the predicate, and the case is
counted as a calibration pair rather than a held-out confirmation; still, the one case
on the visible side was reached by changing the oracle, and a reader may weigh it
accordingly.

\paragraph{One author built the oracles, chose the frames and classified.}
The design holds confounds constant at the price of experimenter independence: the
same person wrote both oracles, fixed the frames and acted as the first rater. The
mechanical frame, the pre-registered batches and the blind second rater limit what
that person could steer; they do not remove the dependence.

\paragraph{The frame's pattern is a proxy.}
``Quotes internal state'' is operationalized as a fixed regular expression over the
fix message; messages that print state in another form are missed, and 437 fixes
narrow to 68 on that proxy. Nothing in the classification depends on the proxy being
complete, but its representativeness for the component's history is not measured.

\paragraph{The contrast is two oracles in one system.}
Confounds are held constant rather than randomized: same corpus, same discipline,
same protocol, same author. That is the strength of the design and also its limit.
Two oracles differing in one respect show that the respect matters here; they do not
establish an effect size, and no claim of one is made.

\paragraph{Calibration establishes a channel, not a class.}
Each pair certifies that a specific oracle either fires or cannot fire on one
specific defect. Generalizing from a pair to a class is an inference the pair does
not license on its own, and in this work the generalization is carried by the
partition rather than by the pairs. Where a pair and the partition disagree the pair
wins, since it is the direct measurement.

\paragraph{The subject is one analysis in one system.}
The eBPF verifier is an abstract interpreter whose defects are unusually well
documented, which is why it can serve as a case study at all. That same property
makes it unrepresentative: most systems do not publish the internal state their
defects corrupt, and applying the procedure elsewhere may be harder for reasons this
case study cannot expose.
